Plex Across the Network

Plex ended up being a smaller piece of this project than the VLAN and DNS work, but it produced one of the more interesting bugs I ran into, mostly because it looked solved and then quietly turned out not to be.

What I was trying to do

The Plex server itself runs on my office gaming PC rather than a dedicated always-on machine, since I only watch something through it occasionally and didn't see the point in dedicating separate hardware to a service I'd use for maybe twenty minutes a week. That PC sits on the trusted network, with Plex given a reserved address on that network so it doesn't move around every time its lease renews. The clients that need to reach it are spread across the network on purpose, my laptop and phone on the trusted side, but also a Google TV sitting out on the IoT VLAN. I wanted Plex to be one of the specific, narrow exceptions the IoT network is allowed to reach, the same TCP port 32400 exception from the firewall page, rather than opening IoT up broadly just to make one streaming app work for one device.

The bug that looked fixed but wasn't

Once the ACL exception for Plex was in place, the Google TV could see the Plex library just fine. Browsing through movies and shows worked without any issue at all. But actually selecting something to play failed every single time with a generic "Error navigating to the item" message. Meanwhile, a phone connected to that same IoT network could stream from Plex without any problem whatsoever, using the same server, over the same network, through the same firewall rule.

That comparison, one IoT device working and one not, on the same network, hitting the same server, is what actually cracked this open. It ruled out the ACL rule being wrong, since the phone proved the path was genuinely open. It ruled out Plex being broadly broken, since the phone worked without any special configuration. The problem was specific to the Google TV, or more precisely, specific to how Plex was treating a client connecting from a different subnet than the server itself.

What I learned

Plex has its own internal concept of what counts as your "local network," completely separate from anything your router or firewall thinks about the same question. If a client connects from a subnet Plex doesn't recognize as local, Plex can start treating that client more like a remote connection than a same-network one, which changes how it negotiates playback behind the scenes. That mismatch can be bad enough that navigation and playback break entirely, even though discovery and library browsing work fine the whole time, since browsing and playback use different parts of Plex's logic.

What I changed

The fix lived entirely inside Plex, not the network. Under Settings, then Server, then Network, there's an advanced field called LAN Networks where you can explicitly list every subnet that should be treated as local, beyond whatever Plex tries to guess on its own. I added the trusted network's subnet, the Main WiFi subnet, and the IoT subnet to that list, and deliberately left the Guest subnet out of it, since there's no reason a guest device passing through for a day should ever be treated as a trusted local Plex client. After restarting the Plex server and the app on the Google TV, playback started working the exact same way it already did for the phone.

Plex traffic paths

      Trusted network (Plex server, reserved address)
           |
           |-- TCP 32400, normal streaming --> Laptop / phone on trusted network
           |
           |-- TCP 32400, ACL exception ------> Google TV on IoT VLAN
                                                 (also needs Plex's "LAN Networks"
                                                  setting to include the IoT
                                                  subnet, or playback breaks
                                                  even though browsing works)

Why it mattered

This is a good companion to the mDNS story from the previous page. Two different services, two completely different reasons the network layer alone wasn't the full picture. mDNS cared about multicast boundaries and whether discovery traffic could cross a VLAN. Plex cared about its own internal idea of what counts as a local subnet, unrelated to anything the router or firewall enforces. Both are cases where fixing the routing and the firewall rules got me most of the way there, and the last piece of the puzzle lived inside the application itself rather than anywhere in the network configuration.
